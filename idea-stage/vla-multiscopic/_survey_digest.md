### ANGLE: vla-arch — VLA inference/temporal structure: the canonical inference loop, single-rate vs hierarchical/dual-system control, action chunking, async inference, and latency.
SOTA: The canonical modern VLA inference loop (pi0/SmolVLA family) is: (1) encode images+language+proprioception in the VLM backbone once; (2) sample noise for an action chunk of horizon H (typically 16-50 steps); (3) run a SMALL number of flow-matching/diffusion integration steps (K=4 to 10) through an action expert that shares self-attention with the backbone, producing the whole chunk in ONE forward pass (~64-73 ms); (4) execute that chunk OPEN-LOOP for ~0.5-1 s; (5) re-run. This replaced the original RT-2/OpenVLA autoregressive loop (7 discrete tokens or more, decoded left-to-right, ~321 ms/step at best, 1-3 Hz), which was too slow and could not chunk cheaply.\n\nTwo orthogonal axes now define the design space. Axis 1 = TEMPORAL DECOUPLING of inference from execution. Synchronous chunking stalls the robot at chunk boundaries while the next chunk computes; the fix is ASYNCHRONOUS inference. SmolVLA implements this as a RobotClient/PolicyServer split: the client drains an action queue at the 30 fps control clock and, when the queue falls below g*n, fires a new observation to the (possibly remote) server to compute the next chunk concurrently — buying ~30% speedup and ~2x throughput. RTC (NeurIPS 2025) makes this smooth and delay-robust by freezing the prefix that is guaranteed to execute during inference and inpainting the remaining suffix, with no retraining; it is the de-facto async-execution primitive for any flow/diffusion VLA, the user's substrate included.\n\nAxis 2 = ARCHITECTURAL HIERARCHY / MULTI-RATE. The field is converging on dual-system designs: a slow 'System 2' VLM for semantics/intent and a fast 'System 1' visuomotor policy for control. GR00T N1 runs System 2 at ~10 Hz and System 1 (DiT flow head, K=4) at 120 Hz, tightly coupled and jointly trained through cross-attention on shared VLM tokens. Helix (Figure, tech report only) pushes this to 7-9 Hz vs 200 Hz over a 35-DoF humanoid. pi0.5 and Hi Robot realize hierarchy at the reasoning level: predict a high-level semantic subtask, then condition the action expert on it (one model in pi0.5, two models in Hi Robot). So today there are at most TWO rates, they are coupled via shared latents or sequential conditioning, and crucially the fast loop NEVER preempts or overrides the slow loop — it only executes what the slow loop already authorized. SmolVLA specifically remains a single-backbone, single-reasoning-rate model whose only temporal sophistication is the async execution queue.

GAPS: No THREE-rate VLA exists. State of the art tops out at two coupled rates (System 2 reasoning + System 1 control). There is no distinct micro reactive loop (~20 ms, pure sensorimotor) below the action-expert chunk loop, and no separate macro cognition loop that fires only on intention change. SmolVLA in particular is effectively single-rate plus an async queue.; No fast REACTIVE INTERRUPT over an executing chunk. Once a chunk (or even RTC's frozen prefix) is committed, the robot executes it open-loop; nothing aborts mid-chunk when a sudden hazard or affordance change appears. Async inference shortens the commit window but still cannot preempt the in-flight action sequence — exactly the AEF-interrupt capability (P5) that is absent.; The slow and fast clocks are COUPLED/JOINTLY-TRAINED (GR00T N1, Helix) or sequentially conditioned (pi0.5, Hi Robot), not a bottom-up/top-down meet-in-the-middle. Perception does not flow up to revise intent while plans flow down; the data path is essentially one-directional top-down conditioning.; UNIFORM compute per step regardless of scene difficulty. Every observation runs the same backbone+K flow steps; there is no adaptive compute allocation (more denoising/attention where the scene is uncertain, less where it is easy). This is the missing P3 (attention = adaptive compute).; Replan/new-chunk timing is fixed by a queue threshold (g*n in SmolVLA) or a fixed open-loop horizon — it is NOT triggered by an information/uncertainty signal. The decision of WHEN to re-infer is clock-driven, not affordance- or surprise-driven.; The action-chunk DISPERSION signal (self-disagreement of the flow head across K sampled chunks), already computed in the user's harness, is unused by any published VLA inference loop. It is a free, fast uncertainty/affordance-mismatch estimate that no canonical loop feeds back into chunk execution, replan timing, or compute allocation.; Visual tokenization is uniform patches in every VLA backbone (SmolVLM2 included); no affordance- or attention-density-based token routing exists at the perception front-end (missing P4/P7).
TRANSFER HOOKS: P1 (three-clock) onto SmolVLA's async stack: keep the existing RobotClient queue as the MICRO clock (33 ms, drains/executes), the flow action-expert chunk as the MESO clock (re-infer on intention/uncertainty rather than fixed g*n), and run the SmolVLM2 backbone re-encode as the MACRO clock fired only on intention change — turning the one-backbone-per-chunk loop into a true micro/meso/macro hierarchy on the user's exact substrate.; P5 (AEF interrupt) onto the chunk-execution gate using the READY-MADE dispersion signal: add a fast learned/threshold pathway that monitors per-frame chunk dispersion and, when it spikes (affordance-effectivity mismatch), PREEMPTS the executing chunk and forces an immediate re-infer — the missing reactive-override over open-loop chunks. This is the most concrete 'change a fundamental method' landing point and uses a signal the harness already produces.; P2 (bottom-up<->top-down at meso) onto pi0.5/Hi-Robot-style hierarchy: instead of one-directional top-down subtask conditioning, let rising dispersion (perception, bottom-up) at the meso loop revise the high-level subtask token (top-down) before the next macro re-encode — make the meso level the reconciliation point rather than a pass-through.; P3 (attention = adaptive compute) onto the flow-matching integration budget: make K (number of flow/denoising steps) and/or backbone layer-skipping ADAPTIVE to dispersion — spend more integration steps and full backbone depth when the chunk is uncertain, fewer when confident. SmolVLA already supports layer-skipping (L/2) and a fixed 10 steps, so this is a direct knob; targets the claimed ~70% compute reduction.; P3/P7 (density routing, topological) onto SmolVLM2 tokenization: replace uniform patch tokens with attention-density / affordance-driven token routing so representational density concentrates on action-relevant, information-rich regions — the visual-tokenization branch of the fundamental-change mandate.; P4/P6 (affordance-centric, embodiment-aware) onto RTC's frozen-prefix: condition WHICH prefix is frozen and HOW MUCH suffix is inpainted on a body-relative affordance/dispersion estimate, so the commit horizon shrinks when affordances are volatile and lengthens when stable — making RTC's currently delay-driven freeze boundary affordance-driven.

### ANGLE: hier-dualsystem: how the VLA field already splits a monolithic policy into multiple temporal/computational RATES (System-1/System-2, fast-slow, generalist-specialist), what rate structure each named system actually uses, and what is still missing relative to the multi-scopic three-clock + affordance-interrupt + adaptive-compute primitives (P1, P2, P3, P5).
SOTA: The VLA field has decisively adopted the dual-system / fast-slow split, and as of mid-2026 it is essentially a solved and converged design pattern. The consensus architecture is TWO rates: a slow internet-pretrained VLM 'System 2' (high-level reasoning / subtask / latent goal) feeding a fast 'System 1' visuomotor or diffusion/flow action head. Concrete realizations: Helix (7-9 Hz VLM -> 200 Hz 80M policy), GR00T N1 (VLM + diffusion-transformer, jointly trained), pi0.5 (one model, high-level subtask then flow-matched actions), Hi Robot (VLM command-generator + pi0 VLA), RoboDual (generalist + diffusion specialist, ASYNCHRONOUS), Fast-in-Slow (System 1 embedded in the VLM's final blocks, asynchronous frequencies, shared weights), and OpenHelix (open survey + reproduction that maps the bridge-design space). The bridge between rates is the main design knob and ranges from discrete language (Hi Robot), to a learnable latent token (LCB <ACT>), to shared transformer features (Fast-in-Slow). Three properties define the current frontier and where novelty now concentrates: (1) ASYNCHRONY -- making the fast loop run while the slow loop is still computing (RoboDual, Fast-in-Slow, and a 2026 wave of async-inference papers); (2) GATING / event-driven scheduling -- firing the slow loop only when needed rather than every step (StreamVLA's completion-state Lock-and-Gate skips reasoning ~72% of steps; Critic-in-the-Loop event-toggles a third 'critic' system); and (3) UNCERTAINTY-CONDITIONED CADENCE -- using a self-derived signal (action entropy in AAC/CVPR'26, action self-attention in AutoHorizon, self-uncertainty in SCALE, per-step correction in A2C2) to decide how long to trust an open-loop chunk and when to re-look/replan. Notably, the strongest single-clock counterexamples (OpenVLA-OFT) remain SOTA on LIBERO (97.1%) with a monolithic fixed-chunk forward pass, showing the rate-split is motivated by latency/generality, not raw success rate. SmolVLA sits in this landscape as a small-VLM + flow-matching action-expert that already supports async inference (backbone slower than action expert) -- i.e. it has the SUBSTRATE for a two-rate split but ships as effectively single-clock open-loop chunking, and its per-frame action-chunk dispersion is precisely the self-disagreement signal the 2026 uncertainty-gating papers are reinventing.

### ANGLE: adaptive-compute (token-pruning / dynamic tokens / dynamic depth in VLMs and VLAs, and how compute is allocated across the visual field and across action chunks)
SOTA: Adaptive compute is now a crowded sub-field for VLAs, but it is almost entirely framed as *efficiency*, not *cognition*. Three mature clusters exist. (1) VISUAL TOKEN PRUNING/MERGING for VLAs: VLA-Pruner (2511.16449), SpecPrune-VLA (2509.05614), SP-VLA (2506.12723), EfficientVLA (2506.10100), Token Expand-Merge (2512.09927), and differentiable pruning (2509.12594) all drop/merge patch tokens to hit a compute budget while retaining ~98% accuracy at ~75% pruning (1.6-2.0x speedup on OpenVLA/OFT/pi0, LIBERO + SimplerEnv). (2) TEMPORAL REDUNDANCY / CACHING / EARLY-EXIT: VLA-Cache (2502.02175, NeurIPS'25) reuses KV for static tokens and recomputes only environmentally-sensitive task-relevant tokens (~1.7x); DeeR-VLA (2411.02359, NeurIPS'24) does dynamic MLLM DEPTH per-situation (5-6x LLM FLOP cut on CALVIN); CEED-VLA (2506.13725) does consistency-distilled early-exit decoding (4x). FAST (2501.09747, Physical Intelligence) is the orthogonal action-side compression (DCT tokenization) that makes autoregressive VLAs viable at high frequency.

### ANGLE: reactive-chunk: action-chunk interruption, replanning, and reactive execution in diffusion/flow-matching VLA policies. The central tension is the reactivity-vs-consistency tradeoff: long action chunks give smooth, temporally-coherent, low-frequency open-loop execution but blind the policy to new observations mid-chunk, so it cannot react to disturbances, moving targets, or its own mistakes. The literature attacks this along three axes: (A) WHEN to replan / what triggers a chunk abort; (B) HOW to splice a new chunk onto an executing one without jerk (boundary continuity under inference latency); (C) HOW to keep within-chunk actions corrected by fresh observations. Crucially for the user's substrate (SmolVLA + LeRobot async), the dominant deployed trigger is NOT content-based: LeRobot async inference and pi0/pi0.5 RTC both replan on a FIXED queue-length schedule (chunk_size_threshold, default ~0.5-0.7: send a fresh observation when the action queue drops below that fraction). There is no learned, fast, affordance/uncertainty-driven INTERRUPT that preempts the chunk when the scene suddenly demands it — this is the open seam where a multi-scopic interrupt (P5) attaches.
SOTA: As of mid-2026 the reactive-chunk problem is essentially solved for the LATENCY/CONTINUITY sub-problem and only partially solved for the TRIGGER sub-problem. (1) Boundary continuity under async inference is the most mature: RTC (inpainting + soft-masked flow guidance, retraining-free, the de-facto standard, shipped in LeRobot) and its successors A2C2 (per-step correction head) and DEFLECT (offline delay-robust DPO) all let a chunk be replaced smoothly while the previous one runs. (2) Within-chunk freshness is handled by lightweight per-step correction modules (A2C2, DCDP) that re-ground stale chunk actions in the newest observation without re-running the heavy backbone — a de-facto two-rate (slow backbone / fast corrector) pattern. (3) The trigger question — WHEN to replan or abort — is where the field is least settled and most fragmented: the deployed default is purely TIME/QUEUE-based (LeRobot async fires a new request when the action queue drops below chunk_size_threshold; RTC replans on a fixed delay schedule), i.e. open-loop in content. Content-based triggers exist but live in separate papers and are mostly bolt-on monitors rather than integrated: Sentinel (temporal-consistency + VLM-progress), INSIGHT (sequence uncertainty/action-variance/attention -> help trigger), Speculative Verification (verifier accept/reject/rollback), AAC (entropy -> chunk length), TRIAGE (aleatoric-vs-epistemic routing). Test-time samplers (BID) give an explicit sticky<->reactive dial but pay a multi-sample cost. The clearest move toward an integrated architecture is multi-rate/hierarchical execution: HiPolicy (3 frequencies in one model, entropy-gated) and Critic-in-the-Loop (cerebellum/brain/critic tri-system with interrupt flags + buffer flush) — these are the in-domain analogues of the locomotion paper's three-clock + AEF-interrupt design, but neither uses a calibrated flow-head dispersion signal as the interrupt, neither tests on LIBERO, and neither preempts a chunk faster than the backbone's own rate. So: continuity = mature; per-step correction = mature; a learned, FAST (sub-backbone-latency), CALIBRATED, AFFORDANCE/UNCERTAINTY-driven INTERRUPT that preempts an executing chunk = still open.

### ANGLE: tta-uncertainty
SOTA: By mid-2026 the field has firmly established three things. (1) VLAs carry usable internal failure/uncertainty signals: SAFE (NeurIPS'25) shows a single scalar from VLA latents predicts failure across tasks, and FAIL-Detect (RSS'25) shows flow-based density / epistemic scalars do the same for diffusion/flow policies. (2) Conformal prediction is the default calibration layer: SAFE, FAIL-Detect, UPS, and ReconVLA all wrap their raw signal in CP to get a distribution-free, statistically-guaranteed threshold — this is now table stakes, not a contribution. (3) Uncertainty is increasingly used to ADAPT execution at inference, training-free: AAC (CVPR'26) and AutoHorizon shrink the chunk/horizon when uncertain (entropy / attention-sink signals), SCALE modulates perception AND action, and Recurrent-Depth VLA adapts COMPUTE depth per step on latent convergence. Separately, dual-system fast/slow VLAs (FiS-VLA, Helix, async fast-slow) implement multi-rate control, and lightweight fast loops over slow chunks exist (A2C2 every-step correction, SV-VLA deviation-based replan). The when-to-ask/defer taxonomy is mature (UPS: execute / ask-language / ask-intervention; ReconVLA: halt+fallback). For the specific substrate here — flow-head action-chunk DISPERSION (self-disagreement across K sampled chunks) as a fast uncertainty signal — the building blocks all exist (FAIL-Detect uses a flow density signal; ReconVLA uses CQR on a flow policy / LIBERO; ensemble disagreement is the textbook uncertainty estimator) but they are used to RANK/FILTER candidate actions, SET a scalar chunk length, or HALT — not to flip the policy into a structurally different operating mode.

### ANGLE: libero-eval
SOTA: LIBERO (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B, arXiv:2306.03310) is the dominant manipulation benchmark for VLA evaluation, with four 10-task suites that isolate transfer of distinct knowledge types: Spatial (same objects, varied layout), Object (same layout, varied object identity), Goal (same scene, varied task goal), and Long/LIBERO-10 (compositional long-horizon).
