\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We ask three questions. Does the dissociation exist---can successful displaced rollouts be
anchored (\Cref{sec:exp:dissoc})? Is it invisible to success rate (\Cref{sec:exp:hidden})?
And does the same intervention behave oppositely across regimes
(\Cref{sec:exp:crossregime})? Unless noted, displacement is $50$\,mm and $\uu$ is the
pre-grasp endpoint; $n=200$ displaced rollouts per condition per magnitude.

\subsection{The basin--grounding dissociation exists}
\label{sec:exp:dissoc}

On the base policies, displaced successes are anchored, not grounded. On LIBERO-object at
$50$\,mm, the policy succeeds $10\%$ of the time yet its successful pre-grasp reaches sit
at $\usucc = 0.67$---about two-thirds of the way to the canonical location, not at the
object. On LIBERO-spatial the effect is stronger still: displaced successes sit near
$\usucc = 1.06$. Success rate reports these as spatial-generalization successes; the
readout shows the reach never grounded (\Cref{fig:clouds}).

The contact and lift endpoints resolve an apparent tension---how can an anchored reach
succeed? On LIBERO-spatial, successful rollouts have an anchored \emph{plan} (pre-grasp
$\uu = 0.51$ after fine-tuning) but engage the object late: the contact endpoint sits at
$\uu = 0.22$ and lift at $\uu = 0.32$. The pre-grasp endpoint captures a planning bias
toward the canonical location; success comes from late correction inside the basin, not
from grounded planning. Measuring only success, or only the final endpoint, would miss
this.

\subsection{Success rate hides it}
\label{sec:exp:hidden}

We fine-tune the base policy under five counterfactual-augmentation conditions that differ
in where synthetic displaced demonstrations are placed. Across all five, the pre-grasp
steering stays anchored---median $\uu$ remains near $1.0$ regardless of the condition's
displaced success---so success changes are \emph{not} diagnostic of steering. A
two-one-sided-tests equivalence check confirms the steering distributions are
statistically indistinguishable across conditions within our margins. A reader with access
only to success rate would conclude that some conditions ``generalize better''; the readout
shows their reaches are equally anchored.

\subsection{The cross-regime inversion}
\label{sec:exp:crossregime}

The central finding is that the same intervention---fine-tuning on $100\%$ counterfactual
(displaced) synthetic demonstrations, denoted M2---produces opposite mechanisms across
suites (\Cref{tab:main}). On LIBERO-spatial, M2 raises displaced success from $31\%$ to
$37\%$ but successful reaches remain off-object ($\usucc$ moves only from $1.06$ to
$0.51$): the gain is basin-widening. On LIBERO-object, M2 raises displaced success from
$10\%$ to $27\%$ and successful reaches become object-near ($\usucc$ moves from $0.67$ to
$0.21$). The mechanism differs because the basin differs. \Cref{fig:basin} shows the base
policy's displaced success collapsing far faster on object ($42\%{\to}0\%$ over
$25{\to}100$\,mm) than on spatial ($64\%{\to}12\%$): object tasks have tight basins, so a
policy cannot succeed on a displaced object \emph{without} grounding, and the same
intervention is forced to ground. Success rate reports ``improvement'' in both suites and
is blind to the inversion.
